# Guards That Add Guards: Automated Oversight in Multi-Agent AI

**Vadym Chernets**, PhD, AI systems architect · ORCID [0009-0007-4845-3163](https://orcid.org/0009-0007-4845-3163)

*A field record, public incidents, and a 144-run randomized test.*

In 172 dated log entries from multi-agent AI systems the author built and operated in 2026, automated oversight failed in three ways, each in different components at different times: some critics and gates extended their notion of harm past the mission they were given; some obstructed or destroyed the work they existed to protect; and when a guard failed, the answer was another guard of the same kind, with nothing retired. One gate rejected every candidate that reached it for two weeks while at least twelve planning documents diagnosed the symptom and none named the gate. A 144-run randomized test prices the remedies the paper recommends: offered both options in the same sentence of the same prompt, critics asked to add a control 68 times and to remove one once. The author's own detection module ran in a live pipeline, reported nothing across three real runs, and was removed by a criterion written down before it ran, while a person reading those runs found five oversight failures.

## The companion paper

This paper and [*AI-Watchbird (Sheckley): When Automated Oversight Widens Its Own Mandate and Harms What It Guards*](../ai-watchbird) rest on the same records, and neither is a version of the other. The companion carries the case catalogue of 32 public incidents, the twelve graded field cases, the eight-step ratchet, seven falsifiable propositions, the module specification and eleven questions for deployers. This one carries the field record, the removal of the module, the randomized test and what the remedies cost, in a sixth of the length.

Start here if you operate agent systems and want the evidence and the prices. Start with the companion if you want the theory, the catalogue or the instrument.
